## Outcome

Completed. The thread's settled points are carried to the suite: a decision log
line now records the alternatives a point had to overcome, under a trigger that
fires on an argument against the alternative rather than on its mere mention,
and the one-directional dissent template that previously covered a slice of this
ground is gone in favour of the general rule.

The run executed from this thread's `log.md` and `glossary.md` directly; the
thread holds no `spec.md`.

## Changes

- `suite/shared/references/formats/log-line.md` — the `decision` type states
  that a point which had to overcome a rejected alternative names it and why in
  the same line, with a second worked example showing the shape. A new rule
  under `## Rules` fixes where the rejection sits, that it carries its reason,
  and that the entry stays one line however many alternatives it names.
- `suite/shared/references/instructions/append-log-line.md` — a new rule carries
  the trigger (settling the point required an argument against the alternative,
  from either side), the exclusions (merely presented, compared, or left
  unselected, whatever its provenance), and the scope limit (it governs what a
  settled point's line carries, never whether a point has settled).
- Eight mirrored copies regenerated by `scripts/sync-shared-references.mjs`
  across `discussion`, `resolve-pending-decisions`, `close-thread`, and `spec` —
  the four skills the manifest declares these two files to.
- `suite/skills/capture-discussion/discussion/SKILL.md` — the peer-framing bullet
  keeps its mandate to flag a point the agent believes is wrong and loses its
  inline log-line template; its closing sentence now says an ADR body carries
  the same rejection rather than the same dissent.

## Deviations

- The run implemented directly from the thread's `log.md` and `glossary.md` —
  departs from the usual `spec.md` input — because the thread holds no spec and
  the invocation asked for implementation straight after the discussion. Every
  change traces to a logged decision or constraint.
- The instruction's new rule carries an explicit scope limit stating that an
  argument against an alternative is not by itself a settled point — departs
  from the logged constraint that this behaviour needed no new rule — because
  that constraint guards existing behaviour against being *displaced* by the new
  trigger, and the limit is a boundary on the new rule rather than a restatement
  of the old one. The corresponding guardrail about option letters was
  deliberately left unwritten, since the transient-framing rule above it already
  covers that with nothing new to add.

## Remaining concerns

- The trigger rests on the agent's judgment of argument versus comparison rather
  than on a mechanical test. This was the deliberate choice over a broader
  mention-based trigger, and it is the part of the rule most likely to drift in
  practice.
- `resolve-pending-decisions` inherits the rule solely through the shared
  instruction and gained no wording of its own, which was the settled reading.
  Whether that is enough is only observable in use.
