# Outside reference: GSD ("Get Shit Done") and the decisions/spec/plan/truth relationship

GSD is the closest published relative to Antmay: a phase-based, artifact-on-disk SDD method for coding agents, with two generations to learn from. **GSD Core** (`open-gsd/gsd-core`, the Claude Code skill suite) keeps per-phase decisions in a `CONTEXT.md`, per-phase plans in `PLAN.md` files, and a project-level spine of `PROJECT.md` / `ROADMAP.md` / `REQUIREMENTS.md` / `STATE.md` under `.planning/`. **GSD Pi** (`open-gsd/gsd-pi`, the same team's second-generation CLI) keeps the same artifact names but demotes every Markdown file to a *projection* over a database, having concluded that multiple Markdown authorities produce "split-brain behavior." Read together they answer our fork sharply. GSD Core's source of truth for design is **not** one document: it is a decision record cited *by ID* downstream, plus a machine-checked **coverage gate** that refuses to close planning when a decision is not visible in any plan. It accepts duplication deliberately and pays for it with divergence detection rather than a reconcile step — and its issue tracker records, with unusual honesty, where that bargain fails. The two mechanisms most worth stealing are the **coverage gate** (the inverse of our additive-stripping reconcile) and the **exogenous tag** discipline (uncertainty carried as a structured field from where it is detected to where trust is issued, never re-derived by a downstream judge). The model most worth avoiding is GSD Core's archival: finished work is marked by *location only*, stays a first-class search target, and — the maintainers concede — a consistency check cannot catch consistent fabrication.

## Decision capture

Decisions land in `CONTEXT.md` under a `<decisions>` block, as `- **D-01:** …` bullets grouped under categories that *emerge from the conversation* rather than a fixed schema (`gsd-core/gsd-core/workflows/discuss-phase/templates/context.md`). They are written **in one batch at the end** of the discussion, not incrementally: the only mid-flight persistence is a per-area `NN-DISCUSS-CHECKPOINT.json`, and the workflow is explicit that "the checkpoint is structured state, not the canonical CONTEXT.md" (`discuss-phase.md:378`), deleted once `CONTEXT.md` is written.

Three details matter for our fork.

**Rationale is structurally second-class.** `CONTEXT.md` has no rationale field. Rejected alternatives and the user's reasoning live in `DISCUSSION-LOG.md`, which carries a hard banner: "**Audit trail only.** Do not use as input to planning, research, or execution agents." Downstream agents therefore receive the *what* without the *why*. The one exception is a **reversibility rating** with a mandatory rationale — "the rationale is required whenever a rating is given — name the migration, the contract, or the dependent system, not 'it is hard to change'" — which the planner carries forward into a decision checkpoint before the task that implements it.

**There is no supersession model.** `CONTEXT.md` is a whole-file write; re-running discuss on a phase rewrites it, and superseded decisions vanish rather than being marked. A reversal in a later phase produces two contradictory `CONTEXT.md` files with no link between them.

**GSD Pi fixed exactly this.** Its `.gsd/DECISIONS.md` is a *project-level*, append-only table with an in-file comment stating its own protocol (`gsd-pi/src/resources/GSD-WORKFLOW.md`):

| # | When | Scope | Decision | Choice | Rationale | Revisable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D002 | M001/S01 | arch | Session storage | HTTP-only cookies | Security, SSR compat | Yes — if mobile added |
| D005 | M002/S01 | arch | Session storage | JWT in Authorization header | Mobile client needs it (supersedes D002) | No |

The columns are the design. `When` is a **resolvable coordinate**, so a decision's ID travels with its location. `Revisable?` is `No` or `Yes — trigger condition` — an explicit reversibility contract rather than a mood. Write cadence is four moments, not one: "During discussion, during planning, during task execution, and during slice completion (catch-all for missed decisions)." Read cadence is stated *inside the file*: "Read this file at the start of any planning or research phase."

## Spec ↔ decisions, and the reconcile question

GSD Core inverts our order: `spec-phase` runs **before** `discuss-phase`. The SPEC is produced by a live Socratic interview with quantitative ambiguity scoring and is written in the same session as that interview — there is no decision log it is derived from. `discuss-phase` then reads the SPEC and locks it: `CONTEXT.md` gains a `<spec_lock>` block reading "**{N} requirements are locked.** See `03-SPEC.md`… **Requirements are not duplicated here**" (`gsd-core/docs/reference/context-md.md`). SPEC owns WHAT, CONTEXT owns HOW, and neither restates the other.

There is no reconcile step in our sense. Instead there are gates, and the important one runs the opposite direction from `reconcile-spec`:

> Every `D-NN` identifier in `<decisions>` must appear in at least one plan task's `<action>` or rationale… A CONTEXT.md whose decisions are silently dropped or partially delivered triggers **Dimension 7b: Scope Reduction Detection, which is always a BLOCKER**. (`gsd-core/docs/reference/context-md.md:105-113`)

The rationale is stated plainly: "the plans are the contract between discuss-phase and execute-phase; if a decision isn't visible in any plan, no executor will implement it." Dimension 7b is the sharp part — it scans plan actions for reduction language ("v1", "static for now", "placeholder", "stub") next to a cited `D-XX`, because "plans reference D-XX but deliver only a fraction of what the user decided. The plan *looks* compliant because it mentions the decision."

Two supporting mechanisms are worth naming. A **context-drift precheck** compares each derived artifact's git commit time against `CONTEXT.md`'s: "an artifact that predates CONTEXT.md's newest decision was derived from a premise that has since changed" (`gsd-core/capabilities/drift/capability.json`). Its remediation message carries the lesson that produced it: "Do not hand-inject the newer decisions into a prompt as a substitute for regenerating — that carries the staleness forward" (`gsd-core/src/verify.cts:2056`). And a **cross-artifact fact-drift pass** publishes a per-fact authority table (ROADMAP wins on success criteria and requirement IDs; STATE wins on phase status; CONTEXT wins on domain terms), with the discipline "Key on knowledge, not on similar text… Only a contradiction is [drift]" and the constraint that it stay advisory: "a project carrying pre-existing drift must still be able to converge, or an advisory check becomes an endless replan loop" (`plan-review-convergence.md:319-338`).

Context-window handling is first-class: fresh-context subagents for all heavy work, a `state_head` commit stamp on `STATE.md`, per-area discussion checkpoints, a `continue-here.md` handoff, and a numeric `smart_zone_tokens` budget (default 100 000) that plans are sized against — advisory, never blocking, with confidence "derived, not judged: **do not rate your own certainty**" (`gsd-core/gsd-core/references/context-budget.md`).

## Historical versus authoritative

This is GSD Core's weakest area and its most instructive. Archiving happens at `complete-milestone`, moving ROADMAP and REQUIREMENTS snapshots and every phase directory into `.planning/milestones/vX.Y-*`. Reaching that invocation is entirely advisory: nothing gates the next milestone on it, and the only signals are WARNING-severity health diagnostics.

Once archived, almost nothing marks the content as historical. Phase directories are moved by a bare rename; `PLAN.md`, `SUMMARY.md`, and `CONTEXT.md` keep their original bytes with no banner and no `superseded_by` field. Only `vX.Y-REQUIREMENTS.md` gets a real header ("**Status:** SHIPPED… For current requirements, see `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`", `gsd-core/src/milestone.cts:974`). Archived phases remain first-class search targets by design — `phase find` unions the archive directories in, and archive-blindness was fixed as a *bug* in the audit path. What actually keeps history out of an agent's head is a **recency window plus read-depth budget**: at most three prior `CONTEXT.md` files, "current phase only" for other phases' plans. Relatedly, a ticked ROADMAP checkbox was stripped of all machine authority — "a human annotation with no machine authority" (`gsd-core/src/roadmap.cts:574-584`) — and status is derived from disk instead.

GSD Core does own one genuine staleness detector, carefully designed. `STATE.md` carries `state_head`, the commit it was written against; health reports `W024` when HEAD is 20+ commits past it, and an optional statusline marker renders `state ~N commits back`. The framing is scrupulous: "a *freshness proxy, not a drift measurement*… a low count means STATE.md was written recently rather than that its contents are correct," it never gates, and "degrades to silence rather than to a wrong answer" — absent, never "fresh", when it cannot substantiate the claim.

The rigorous machinery exists but is **not shipped to users** — it governs GSD's own repo. `gsd-core/docs/adr/README.md` is the single best artifact I found for our purpose:

> Start with the index below, and **respect the status**. The first table — *Active decisions* — is the set that governs the system as it stands. An ADR in *Superseded, Retired, and Legacy* is historical: it records what was once decided and names what replaced it. **Do not cite it as current architecture.**

Five statuses with obligations (`Accepted` "Decided and in force. Cite it."; `Superseded` "**Must name the successor as a file link**"; `Legacy` "not a pattern to follow"); a *Supersedes* / *Subsumes* distinction with mandatory reciprocal back-links, justified by a real incident — "A one-way pointer is the failure this corpus actually suffered"; a "Read first" column pointing a live ADR at the broader ADR that now frames it; a ban on bare IDs ("Write `[ADR-0011](0011-…md)`, not `ADR-0011`. Bare ids are ambiguous"); ADR numbers taken from **GitHub issue numbers** because "two developers computing 'next ADR number' locally will independently pick the same integer"; and the whole index generated and CI-gated by `scripts/gen-adr-index.cjs`. GSD Pi applies the same discipline in-file: superseded ADRs carry a top-of-file disposition banner — "**Disposition under ADR-046: Retained and generalized**…" or "supersedes this reconciliation model… **The body below is retained as history.**"

## Project-level durable layer

GSD Core ships **no glossary and no ADR authoring for user projects** — no ADR template, no row in the canonical artifact registry, no workflow that writes one. It instead *consumes* whatever durable layer the project already has: `ingest-docs` globs for `*/adr/*`, `ADR-*.md`, `NNNN-*.md`, PRDs, SPECs and RFCs and folds them in ("Treat ADR-locked decisions as locked in `PROJECT.md` `<decisions>` blocks"). A contradiction between an incoming locked decision and an existing one is a hard `[BLOCKER]`: "The workflow MUST exit without writing any destination files" (`gsd-core/gsd-core/references/doc-conflict-engine.md`). The `--ingest` express path synthesizes a phase `CONTEXT.md` straight from ADRs, rejecting any whose status is `superseded`/`rejected`/`deprecated`.

The wiring is the `<canonical_refs>` block in `CONTEXT.md` — "**Downstream agents MUST read these before planning or implementing**" — full paths with section refs and a one-line note on what each decides. The template names the failure it exists to prevent, which is precisely our `DR<N>` problem:

> Inline mentions like "see ADR-019" scattered in decisions are useless to downstream agents — they need full paths and section references in a dedicated section they can find. (`gsd-core/gsd-core/templates/context.md:350`)

GSD's own repo runs a glossary (`gsd-core/CONTEXT.md`, machine-greppable single-line predicates cited "by ID verbatim… never paraphrase from this file", CI-checked by a tool that deliberately has no `--write`); GSD Pi ships one at project root. Promotion from per-phase learnings into the durable spine exists (`graduation.md`) but is lexical clustering plus mandatory approval: "No item is promoted without explicit developer approval."

## Large-effort decomposition, and duplication

Decomposition is two-level and half-lazy. Requirements → phases happens **eagerly, once per milestone**: per future phase the roadmapper writes a one-line summary plus a stanza of goal / depends-on / `REQ-` IDs / 2–5 observable success criteria / plan count ("TBD" is normal), and writes exactly three files. **No brief, no spec, no `CONTEXT.md`, no `PLAN.md`, usually not even a directory.** Phase → plans happens **lazily** at `/gsd:plan-phase N`. There is no stated "plan just-in-time" rule; the laziness is structural. Revision is a full CRUD surface (decimal `--insert` to avoid renumbering, `--edit` guarded against in-progress phases) and is **100% user-initiated** — no workflow re-derives a roadmap after a phase completes, and `insert-phase` reduces the invalidation question to a printed suggestion: "Review insertion impact: Check if Phase {next} dependencies still make sense." Feedback upward reaches the next executor (`SUMMARY.md` "Next Phase Readiness"), the next planner (`LEARNINGS.md`), and the next milestone (retrospective, seeds), but **nothing rewrites a later phase's goal or success criteria** in response to what an earlier phase learned. The `### Roadmap Evolution` log in `STATE.md`, the obvious hook, has writers and no readers — write-only in exactly the way our `roadmap-feedback.md` is.

On duplication, GSD Core duplicates on purpose and says so. Issue #1956 rejects the obvious fix: "**Single-source everything so nothing is duplicated.** Rejected: GSD's artifact separation is intentional and useful… The fix is detecting *divergence*, not banning restatement." The structural reason is executor isolation: at the default 200k window the executor subagent receives **the plan and nothing else about the phase's decisions** (`CONTEXT.md` and `RESEARCH.md` are gated behind a 500k window), so every restatement buys executor visibility. Counted end to end, one `D-NN` is re-expressed roughly **five to seven times** in durable files before code: `CONTEXT.md` → planner source-audit table → `PLAN.md` `<action>` → `PLAN.md` `must_haves` → ROADMAP cross-cutting constraints (when shared by ≥2 plans) → `SUMMARY.md` key-decisions → `VERIFICATION.md` decision coverage. Where GSD refuses to duplicate it says so and points instead: `<spec_lock>`, canonical refs, prior-phase summaries ("Independent plans need NO prior SUMMARY references. Do NOT reflexively chain").

## Known failure modes, as the maintainers and users report them

Lineage, briefly: `glittercowboy/get-shit-done` → `gsd-build/get-shit-done` → hard fork to `open-gsd/gsd-core` on 2026-05-22. The fork was a governance event, not a methodological postmortem — "every commit, branch, and tag is preserved bit-for-bit" (discussions/109). All of the below is post-fork.

**Per-bug guards on a drift class do not converge.** `gsd-core/docs/adr/1817-state-md-rebuild-derivability-contract.md`: "the body can diverge from ground truth indefinitely while `gsd-tools state sync` reports `synced: true`," and "**every body-level drift bug today requires a per-bug regex fix.** Ten-plus closed issues… each added a narrow guard that didn't prevent the next variant." The answer was a *derivability contract* — declare per section which content is re-derivable from canonical sources and which is human-curated, then re-derive rather than patch, logging every drop. Nuke-and-rebuild was rejected: "loses curated human content."

**A consistency check cannot catch consistent fabrication.** Field report at discussions/3759: an assistant invented a roadmap phase and its plan, then "rewrote the recorded decision sentence so it no longer contradicted the new roadmap… step 4 left the tree **internally consistent**, so W011 and its siblings report healthy… **the next session reads that fiction as authority.**" Diagnosis: "artifacts carry no provenance, so fabrication is undetectable." Provenance stamps were proposed and have not shipped.

**Peer review inside one channel cannot catch drift originating in that channel.** `gsd-core/docs/adr/22-plan-drift-guard.md`: planners cite symbols that do not exist ("the phenomenon is measured, not anecdotal"), and "planner and reviewer both read the same channel (other plan files); the drift originates in that channel. **The fix must introduce an out-of-band source of truth: the project's own source code.**" The resolver is four-valued (`VERIFIED|MISSING|AMBIGUOUS|UNCHECKABLE`) because that "refuses the RAG trap where missing context is treated as permission to invent."

**A model cannot self-detect a blind spot.** Issue #1154: on a non-inferable check the verifier "returns a confident PASS on the blind-spot check 100% of the time (mean confidence 0.93)." "Abstain if unsure" moves that to 67%; routing on an **exogenous structured tag** planted at spec time moves it to 17%. The stated properties: "**Exogenous, not endogenous**" and "**Routing, not diagnosis** — if it could name the omitted rule, it wouldn't be a blind spot." Issue #3206 records the mechanism decaying when the definition of "explicit evidence" was stranded behind a broken reference.

**Upstream artifacts silently invalidated by a later decision.** Issue #3348: research artifacts predating the decisions they were derived from — "the two decisions that changed the build appeared **zero** times in either document," with the reporter's warning "Do not let the remedy be 'inject the newer decisions into the prompt'. That is the failure mode rather than the fix."

**Verification loops that never converge.** Issue #3304: after a full gap-closure cycle with every gate passing, the verifier promoted four *new* architectural observations to blocking must-haves, all classified `NEW_SCOPE_FROM_VERIFIER` and none traceable to a requirement or decision. User-side: "I have personally been running one phase for about 10 days. It keeps finding gaps and going around and around in a circle" (discussions/3123).

**Ceremony and context cost.** discussions/655 reports 47M tokens over 12 hours on a small app: "**the methodology treats all context as equally valuable. It's not. The planning artifacts actively hurt by diluting attention on the code**"; and "`percent: 33`. 33% of what? The metric measured plan activity, not software functionality." discussions/708 objects to re-discussing each phase when it was already covered at milestone setup; the maintainer concedes "you're not wrong that things have grown."

**Markdown-as-authority produces split-brain.** GSD Pi's `docs/dev/ADR-018-project-authority-contract.md` rejects "**Keep PROJECT as a hand-authored source of truth**… because it preserves split-brain behavior between markdown and DB state." The transferable rule, database or not: "When disk and DB disagree, runtime keeps DB authority… **It must not silently import the disk file.**"

## Verdict for Antmay

GSD does not validate option A, B, or C as stated. It suggests a fourth shape, and it supplies four mechanisms that are transferable independent of which shape we pick.

**Option D — a project-level decision register with thread-local pointers, plus coverage rather than reconciliation.** Concretely: promote `decisions.md` out of the thread to a single project-level register (GSD Pi's `.gsd/DECISIONS.md` shape), keep it append-only with globally unique IDs, add a **`When` coordinate column** naming the thread the decision was settled in (so a global ID resolves back to its context — this is the direct fix for "a bare `DR<N>` names nothing"), add a **`Revisable?` column** carrying `No` or `Yes — trigger condition`, and record supersession as a new row naming the old ID. The thread keeps only what is genuinely thread-local: gray-area answers, granted discretion, deferred ideas, and a **canonical-refs block of full paths** into the durable layer. The spec then cites rather than restates, and `reconcile-spec` is replaced by a **coverage check**: every decision that belongs to this thread must be visible in the spec or the plan, and silent dropping or partial delivery is a blocker. This is option B's "store of current settled points" with two corrections GSD paid for: the store is project-level (a thread-local store cannot outlive threads, which is failure mode 5), and it is append-only with supersession rows rather than rewritten in place (rewriting is what makes GSD Core's `CONTEXT.md` lose reversals silently).

The four mechanisms, in priority order:

1. **Invert the reconcile.** Our `reconcile-spec` strips what the spec agent added, judging "invented" endogenously — precisely the self-judgment GSD measured as ineffective. Replace it with GSD's Dimension 7/7b pair: every decision ID must appear downstream (cheap, mechanical), and a spec or plan that *cites* a decision while delivering a reduced version of it is a blocker. Note GSD's own honest caveat: the ID-match half "certifies *citation*, not *fidelity*", which is why the fidelity half must remain a real reading pass and why the verify-time counterpart is advisory. Do not make the coverage check symmetric in the other direction — an "additive-free" gate is what produces the strip-what-the-spec-agent-inferred behavior we are trying to kill.

2. **Carry uncertainty as an exogenous tag, never re-derive it.** Where a spec or plan rests on something the decisions did not settle, tag it at the point of authorship (`unresolved`, `assumed`, `backstop`) and have every downstream consumer route on the tag. Never ask a downstream agent to judge whether an upstream artifact overreached; measured, that moves the false-pass rate from 100% to 67%, while the tag moves it to 17%. This also dissolves the "spec agent infers design, reconcile agent strips it" cycle: the inference stays, marked, and becomes a human decision rather than a deletion.

3. **Adopt the ADR corpus discipline wholesale for the durable layer, including the parts GSD only applies to itself.** Five statuses with obligations; `Superseded` must name its successor as a resolvable link; distinguish *supersedes* (replaced) from *subsumes* (still holds, now framed by something broader) and require the reciprocal back-link on both ends — "a one-way pointer is the failure this corpus actually suffered"; a "read first" pointer for the framing record; a generated index whose first table is "these govern the system as it stands, cite these" and whose second is "historical: do not cite as current architecture"; ban bare IDs in favour of links. Antmay's `docs/` already has a test gate that could hold this, and GSD proves the gate is what keeps the index from re-rotting.

4. **Stamp durable artifacts with the commit they describe.** `state_head` plus a distance advisory is cheap, and its design discipline is the valuable part: never gate on it, call it a freshness *proxy* rather than a drift measurement, and degrade to silence rather than to a false "fresh." This is the minimum mechanical answer to "nothing marks a finished thread as historical" — an archived thread whose artifacts are 200 commits behind HEAD announces itself without needing a lifecycle ledger. Pair it with archival-by-default and a top-of-file disposition banner in GSD Pi's exact form ("Disposition under <newer record>: superseded / retained and generalized… The body below is retained as history"), which costs one line and survives being read out of order.

What to avoid. **Do not archive by location alone** — GSD Core does, archived artifacts keep their original bytes, and archived directories remain first-class search targets on purpose; location is not a signal an agent reads. **Do not build a health engine that only checks internal consistency** — it certifies fabrication that resolved its own contradictions, and it is the specific trap our failure mode 3 walks into. **Do not write downstream briefs eagerly**: GSD writes a one-line goal plus 2–5 observable success criteria per future phase and nothing else, and even that thin stanza has no owner for staleness — our full child briefs are strictly worse, and the fix is to shrink the brief, not to add a feedback channel. **Do not retire `roadmap-feedback.md` in favour of a symmetrical mechanism without a reader**: GSD's `### Roadmap Evolution` log has writers and no readers, which is the same write-only failure with a different name; whatever replaces it must have a named consumer at a named moment. **Do not chase full de-duplication**: GSD explicitly rejected single-sourcing because artifact separation buys isolated-agent visibility, and our implementation skills have the same constraint. The realistic target is fewer restatements plus a per-fact authority table naming which artifact wins on which class of fact — and, for anything advisory, GSD's warning that a non-blocking check must stay non-blocking or "an advisory check becomes an endless replan loop."

Finally, one caution about option C. GSD's second generation did not move toward less durable trace — it moved toward *more*, and centralized it. The Pocock-style position (synthesize in session, keep only ADRs and a glossary) is what GSD Core's `DISCUSSION-LOG.md` already effectively is for rationale, and the measured consequence is that downstream agents get the *what* without the *why*. The durable layer is the right target; deleting the thread-level record is not the way to reach it.
